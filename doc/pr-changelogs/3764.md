 * Add an opt-in HDR scene target: with `PBRSceneTarget = hdr` (default `direct`) the world is drawn into a floating-point colour target and then shown through one display pass; if the target cannot be allocated, the engine falls back to `direct`
   * `PBRDisplayTransform` picks the display curve: `khronos-neutral` (default), `agx`, `aces`, `aces-hill`, `hable`, `uchimura`, `reinhard`, `reinhard-jodie`, `filmic-hejl`, `filmic-toe-shoulder`, `bar-custom`, `legacy` (no curve), `linear-debug`; `PBRExposureEV` scales the scene by 2^EV before the curve; `PBRDisplayDither = triangular` (default) adds a 1/255 grain against banding, `off` leaves it out
   * `PBRDisplayBoundary = after-world` (default) presents before the screen-space effects, so they still draw into the window; `after-screen-effects` presents after them, so they are tone mapped too
   * `PBRSceneDepthFormat = d24s8` (default; depth and stencil are blitted into the window, or depth only by a shader where the blit is refused) or `d32fs8` (depth is copied by a shader, stencil is not copied); `PBRHDRColorFormat = rgba16f` (default) or `r11g11b10f` (half the memory, no alpha)
   * `gl.SceneTargetPBRMask(true/false)`: while on, every fragment shader drawn must write output location 1 (`1.0` for PBR colour, `0.0` for display-referred colour); the display pass applies the curve only to pixels marked `1.0`, so legacy-shaded pixels keep their look. Returns false and does nothing outside the HDR world phase
   * `$scene_color` and `$scene_depth`: texture names for `gl.Texture` and material texture tables. During the world phase they are the scene drawn so far (colour as the display pass reads it: sRGB-encoded, extended above 1.0; depth with stencil); with MSAA they are resolved single-sample copies. They are 0 with `PBRSceneTarget = direct`
   * With MSAA, `gl.CopyToTexture`, `gl.ReadPixels`, `gl.SaveImage` and `gl.BlitFBO(nil, ...)` resolve the scene before reading it
   * New `Spring.GetSceneTargetInfo` and `Spring.TestDisplayTransforms` (see their docs)
